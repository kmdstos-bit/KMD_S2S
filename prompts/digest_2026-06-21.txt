Forecast summary for 21 June 2026:

A persistent dry signal across the western half of the country contrasts with an emerging wet trend in the eastern highlands and coastal areas.

HIGHLANDS WEST OF THE RIFT VALLEY, RIFT VALLEY AND LAKE VICTORIA BASIN, AND NORTHWESTERN KENYA:
These regions are characterized by a significant dry signal. Northwestern Kenya is most severely affected, with p33 reaching 88% in week 1 (p50anom=-71%, -6.0mm) and remaining consistently dry through week 6. The Highlands West and Rift Valley follow a similar, albeit slightly less extreme trend, with dry anomalies persisting through week 4 and signals suggesting this continues into the long-range period.

HIGHLANDS EAST OF THE RIFT VALLEY:
This region shows a distinct wet tendency. In week 1, 33% of grid cells exceed an EFI of 0.5 (p50anom=+29%, +2.4mm). This signal is expected to strengthen, with p50anom values climbing to +87% (+6.2mm) by week 6.

COAST:
After a dry start in week 1 (p33=70%, p50anom=-36%, -3.5mm), the forecast indicates a transition to wetter conditions from week 3 onwards, with p50anom reaching +39% (+2.8mm) by week 5.

NORTHEASTERN KENYA AND SOUTHEASTERN LOWLANDS:
Both regions exhibit a dry start in week 1, with the Southeastern Lowlands showing a p33 of 64% (p50anom=-50%, -2.0mm). These areas trend toward near-neutral or slightly above-normal rainfall from week 3 onwards, though signals remain moderate.

Overall, the six-week outlook suggests a robust divide between a persistently dry west and a progressively wetter eastern corridor, with the most significant deviations from climatology occurring in the northwest and eastern highlands.
