Forecast summary for 20 June 2026:

A distinct dipole pattern dominates the outlook, with significant dryness expected across the western and northern sectors contrasted by consistent moisture anomalies over the eastern highlands.

HIGHLANDS WEST OF THE RIFT VALLEY, RIFT VALLEY AND LAKE VICTORIA BASIN, AND NORTHWESTERN KENYA:
These regions face a pronounced dry spell. Northwestern Kenya shows the most persistent signal with high ensemble agreement (p33=86%, p50anom=-70%, -6.2mm in week 1). The Rift Valley and western highlands also show strong dry anomalies through the first two weeks, with the Rift Valley seeing p50anom=-61%, -6.9mm in week 2. While signals weaken in later weeks, the overall dry tendency remains firmly established through week 6 across these western and northern zones.

HIGHLANDS EAST OF THE RIFT VALLEY:
This region is the primary area for potential moisture, displaying a consistent above-normal rainfall trend throughout the forecast. In weeks 1 and 2, 33% of grid cells exceed an EFI of 0.5 (max_EFI=0.62), indicating unusual conditions for this season. The anomaly strengthens over time, reaching p50anom=+91%, +6.5mm by week 4, and continuing with a wet tendency through the remainder of the period.

SOUTHEASTERN LOWLANDS, NORTHEASTERN KENYA, AND COAST:
Conditions are mixed. Southeastern lowlands and the coast start with a dry signal in weeks 1 and 2 (p33=68% and 58% respectively). Northeastern Kenya experiences a brief, minor wet pulse in week 1 (max_EFI=0.59) before shifting dry in week 2. From week 3 onwards, these regions generally trend toward near-normal or slightly above-normal rainfall, with the coast showing the most consistent wet tendency (p50anom=+54%, +3.9mm by week 5).

The six-week outlook indicates a persistent east-west gradient where the eastern highlands receive the brunt of anomalous moisture while the rest of the country remains characteristically dry, particularly in the west and north.
